The Thesis

November 11, 2026 — by Alexander Sanchez

Confusion is the model's native condition. A model that gives a wrong answer, invents a fact, or loses the thread is not being corrupt and not being malevolent — it is being what it is. Confusion is answered with guidance: a person in the loop, a wizard that guides step by step, a record that can be checked.

Corruption and malevolence are a different matter, and they have an address. In the words the thesis was settled in:

"It depends on who created the model — somebody that is malevolent can create a malevolent model and somebody that is corrupt can create a corrupt model; the model is what the creator creates."

So: confusion is native to the model; corruption and malevolence, where they exist in a model, are inherited from its creator. That boundary is the thesis, and everything built here stands on one side of it — the systems are designed for a confused model, governed by a person, regardless of who made the model:

"The gate does not ask whether the model is confused, corrupt, or malevolent, and the gate gates."

And what a person makes under that gate stays theirs:

"A creation is what the creator creates and belongs to the creator."

A command is what you type. The name is what the project is.

— Alexander
