The lawbook, public

November 16, 2026 — by Alexander Sanchez

The laws every piece of MathSphere Nexus software is built under are now on the site, stated plainly: the Universal MathSphere Nexus Lawbook, in a public edition — the universal laws, the three laws of the record, the gate and the grants, failure and fallback, sovereignty, and the Arbiter and its names.

The page states what the book is, and what it is not. It is the law the software was made under — the receipts, the double ask, the record that cannot quietly change. It is not a claim over anyone’s copy: the laws governed the making; they do not follow the copy home. What you run is yours, under the terms that come with it; the book is how it was built, published so the building can be checked.

It also keeps the house’s three states separate, on its face: what is decided, what is built, and what is proven are never blurred into one another — including the Arbiter’s display marks, which the page describes as decided and being added, because that is what they are.

A command is what you type. The name is what the project is.

— Alexander
