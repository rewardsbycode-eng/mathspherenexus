The Three Rungs — the ladder, run end to end

November 16, 2026 — by Alexander Sanchez

Two days, one prototype, run to the end. The Three Rungs is the advance build of the Ladder — the checking structure the Wizard Central Pro REPL will carry (roadmap Phase 10): a deterministic rung that decides whether code passes, two model rungs that advise and never veto, a gate that is the only write path, budgets enforced at the grant, and exits that hand work back labeled.

It went in slices, each proven before the next began. Rung 1 alone was closed at 10 of 10 — deterministic across 100 runs and two machines. The gate passed its six scenarios twice. The reviewing rung and the fit rung each passed live, and the fit rung’s first live answer broke its own contract — it echoed the template instead of answering — and was receipted as no verdict, raw answer preserved. That receipt is the design working: a model off-contract is recorded, not obeyed and not smoothed over.

Then the trials: 30 programs written by one model, judged by the decider, by a different seat, and by the generator’s own model. The different seat’s five extra fails were adjudicated one by one — all five were false alarms, two of them different invented defects for programs that were byte-for-byte identical. A second run with a larger seat removed the noise entirely. And then the ground-truth check ran every candidate and tested what it printed: 30 correct, 0 defective. There had been nothing to catch. Scored against truth, the larger seat holds the best record of any seat — and the decider itself refused two correct programs over the order of their import lines, a strictness the record shows rather than hides.

Budgets and exits closed the build: a grant refusing a review before the call, and a failed repair loop handing back its last candidate labeled failed, with all nine tries on record. The full account — claims, slices, tables, and what the prototype does not prove — stands on the Three Rungs prototype page. What remains is the paper: the run records, and the rulings. The measurement is done.

A command is what you type. The name is what the project is.

— Alexander
