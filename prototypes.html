Prototypes

Proofs of shape, kept in one place — every prototype ever built in this house, filed under MathSphere Nexus and its project.

A prototype here is a small working build of a design: made to be run and tested, not merely described. It proves a shape — that the design's moving parts fit and behave the way the design says. It is not the product, and this page never calls it one; the product builds stand in the roadmap, in their order.

One rule governs what may be stored here: a prototype must pass house, or it doesn't count as evidence. Passing house means the house laws hold inside it — actions written to a hash-chained receipts record, destructive actions asking twice, the project's own code discipline, and every feature tested live with a document a reader can check. A sketch that fails is a sketch, and it is labeled one or left off.

Every entry states the day its prototype went into implementation and carries its Terminal Record: the dated terminal proof of its tests, parsed in full — how to use everything that was tested and proved. Where no such record exists for a prototype, the entry says so; the absence is shown, never papered over.

Many People, One Machine — The Floor

Prototype · Went into implementation: November 13, 2026 · Tested the same day · 21 of 21 checks passing (19 in the first run; the export boundary was sealed in review the same day)

The floor of the multi-user design: how one installation serves several people with no accounts and nothing to download. An author field on every project, a hash-chained user log read by author, a chat lifecycle that archives and eventually deletes chats — and only chats — and deletion rights with edges: a person deletes their own chats; a project is removed only by the owner's act, its creator's work exported first.

The Hub — Approved Users and Their Projects

Prototype · Went into implementation: November 13, 2026 · Tested the same day · 18 of 18 checks passing

Layer 2 over the floor: the owner approves people onto the installation — they are his responsibility — and they work in projects. The author field fills itself from the sign-in; every project carries its own password, and it opens for no one without it — the owner included: existence is his, contents are the creator's. Removing a person kills their session in the same motion, and their projects survive them.

The Broker

Prototype · Went into implementation: November 13, 2026 · Tested the same day · 17 of 17 checks passing

The gate-side mechanism the whole guide design stands on — in the roadmap it is Phase 7, its own phase. Grants name a context and a scope; handles are keys cut for one grant, and a read succeeds only through a live handle, in scope, in context. Substitution is refused in both directions — even under a misissued grant — and revocation kills every live handle at once. Default-closed throughout, every refusal receipted.

The Start Lock — A Voluntary Door

Prototype · Went into implementation: November 13, 2026 · Tested November 13–14 · 26 of 26 checks passing · Run by the owner on his own machine · Design adopted November 14, 2026

An installation may open with a password door — and the door is voluntary: offered once, at setup, with the honest trade stated before the choice (real security on one side; lose the password and the keys and there is no back door, on the other). Decline, and you are never asked again — though the lock stays findable inside. Setting it mints five one-time recovery keys, the Arbiter's pattern: shown once, hashes only kept; a key opens the door once and is spent, and inside, keys new mints a fresh set that kills the old one. Every step explains itself, and every act is receipted.
