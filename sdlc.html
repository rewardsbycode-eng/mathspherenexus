The wheel we work by

November 14, 2026 — by Alexander Sanchez

Today we put our development cycle on the record. It stands now on the About page as a wheel of six puzzle pieces: Requirements & Formula, Algorithm & Architect, Calculate & Build, Verify & Model Test, Rollout & Operate, and Maintain & Support — and then back around, because the loop does not close.

We are printing it as a description, not an aspiration. Every stage names something that already happens here, and a reader can check each one against the record this site already carries. Requirements and decisions are written down before code moves, and this house keeps three states separate — decided, built, and proven — because confusing them is how software learns to lie. Designs are settled on paper and, where they can be, proven as small prototypes first; the Prototypes page is that stage, kept. The build is one hand: the author writes and commits every line himself, and collaborators draft and verify. Verification runs on a reference machine and against real models, weakest first — the Demo page holds those runs, including the ones where the model failed and the gate refused. What rolls out carries its evidence with it. And when something goes wrong, it goes into an open deviation log, in plain words, and the fix is verified the same way new work is.

The wheel itself arrived with one stage misspelled. Review caught it, the lettering was corrected, and the red was set to this house’s own seal-red before the picture was allowed near the site. The Verify stage caught its own picture; that is the stage working as drawn.

This is what we do, and it is what we will continue to do. The wheel is on the About page so the procedure stands next to the company it describes — and so that anyone, including us, can hold the work up against it.

A command is what you type. The name is what the project is.

— Alexander
